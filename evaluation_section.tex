\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
The theorem says nothing about the size of the automaton, or about how it compares with an
independent implementation. This section measures the automaton
$\mathsf{compile}(f)=\mathsf{compile\_with}(A)$, where $A$ is the automaton that
Spot returns for $\T(f)$. It is obtained by the translation $\T$, Spot,
relaxation, and reset completion, before any further processing, and no
optimization is applied to it here. We ask three questions. Q1: how large is
$\mathsf{compile}(f)$, compared with the automaton of CASAAL~\cite{casaal_tool},
which implements the construction of Li et al.~\cite{li_spin7}? Q2: on
concrete formulas, do the languages agree with CASAAL's on the common restricted model
(strictly increasing timestamps, mutually exclusive events), as far as a
separately implemented timed-automaton checker, or evaluation on words, can
tell? Q3: does the backend satisfy,
on these formulas, the contract that the theorem assumes? The answers are
measurements and tests.

\runinhead{Summary of outcomes.}
Proved: the Rocq development of Section~\ref{sec:correctness}. For Q1, the number of clocks of $\mathsf{compile}(f)$
coincides with CASAAL's on every exactly encoded configuration except F2; locations
equal CASAAL's on N, F3b and F9--F11 and are fewer on F14 (7 against 8),
while locations and transitions are larger on most other configurations, for example 48 locations and
1625 transitions against 16 and 80 for R4 (R7: 576 locations and 296875
transitions against 128 and 1024), a growth that appears to come from Spot's
automaton (Section~\ref{subsec:compile-size}). For
Q2, the TChecker products $P_1$, $P_2$, $S$
(Section~\ref{subsec:tchecker}) are empty on every configuration that completes
(22 of the 31, two of them checked only for $S$) and on all 100 random
formulas, which gives inclusion of our language in CASAAL's, provided that CASAAL's two
automata are complementary (not checkable in general: universality of a nondeterministic
timed B\"uchi automaton is undecidable~\cite{alur_dill94}). For Q3, no discrepancy with
ltl2ba is found on 28 of the 31 configurations. Inconclusive: the larger
configurations (R4--R8, N5--N8 for TChecker; R7, R8, N8 for the backend check)
exceed the time limits and are reported as such.

\subsection{Setup}
The tool \texttt{mtl2tba} parses a formula, computes $\T(f)$, calls Spot~2.16
(\texttt{ltl2tgba}), reads its LBTT output, and applies relaxation and reset
completion. The functions $\T$, relaxation, and reset completion are OCaml
code extracted from the Rocq definitions; the parser, the call to Spot, the
LBTT reader, the printers, and extraction itself are not verified. The clocked-LTL
formula is exactly $\T(f)$ as defined in Section~\ref{sec:translation}.
The negated event atom $\mathsf{LNAct}(a)$ is printed for Spot as the negated
literal \texttt{!"a"} of the atom of $a$, and
\texttt{-compile-only} stops after reset completion, so that the reported time is
that of $\mathsf{compile}(f)$.
Measurements were taken on an Intel Core i7-8650U (1.9~GHz, 11.7~GB RAM) under
Ubuntu~26.04 in WSL~2, with a limit of 600~s per tool run and per TChecker
check (120~s for random formulas); the backend-contract runs of Spot and
ltl2ba used 300~s, and their equivalence checks 600~s. The 31 configurations are fifteen
specification patterns F1--F14 (F3b is F3 without the hat), conjunctions R$k$ of
$k$ bounded responses $\Box(a_i\Rightarrow\Diamond_{\le5}b_i)$ for
$k=1,\dots,8$, and nestings N$k$ of $k$ bounded eventualities
$\Diamond_{\le5}(p_1\land\Diamond_{\le5}(p_2\land\cdots))$. Scripts, inputs,
CASAAL outputs, and raw results are in the artifact
(\path{evaluation/}).

\subsection{Size of compile(f) and CASAAL reference}
\label{subsec:compile-size}
Table~\ref{tab:patterns} lists the patterns F1--F14, and
Table~\ref{tab:compiled} gives, for each configuration, the reachable
locations, transitions, and clocks of $\mathsf{compile}(f)$, and the size of
CASAAL's output. CASAAL was run with mutually exclusive events, which matches
our one-event-per-position words. The two counts of transitions are not
directly comparable: CASAAL labels a transition with a Boolean formula over
propositions, whereas Spot's output, and hence $\mathsf{compile}(f)$, has one
transition per cube over the propositions and clock literals. In R4, for
example, $\mathsf{compile}(f)$ has 48 locations and 1625 transitions, and
CASAAL 16 locations and 80 symbolic transitions; both use four clocks.
Column Grp.~T counts the transitions of $\mathsf{compile}(f)$ after grouping
edges with the same source, target, guard, and resets into one edge labeled by
the union of their events; it is a way of counting and changes no automaton.
Grouping reduces R4 from 1625 to 768 and N8 from 166 to 54 transitions, but a
gap remains (CASAAL: 80 and 19), so the difference is not explained by the
representation of labels alone.

The number of clocks coincides with CASAAL's on every configuration that
CASAAL encodes exactly, except F2: under mutual exclusion,
$\Box(p\Rightarrow\Box_{\le3}q)$ forbids $p$ altogether, and CASAAL produces a
one-state automaton without a clock, whereas $\mathsf{compile}(f)$ keeps two
locations and one clock. This agrees with the clock discipline of the
construction: one clock per structurally distinct primitive timed
subformula. Locations and transitions are larger than CASAAL's on most
configurations, sometimes by a large factor (R4, R5); locations equal CASAAL's
on N, F3b and F9--F11, and are fewer on F14. The growth in the R family appears
to come from Spot's automaton for the conjunction of $k$ clocked-LTL clauses,
before relaxation (Spot's automata for R4--R6 have 768, 3584, and 16384
transitions). On R4 to R7, Spot's translation dominates the time up to
$\mathsf{compile}(f)$: Spot takes 0.12, 2.1, 5.4, and 90~s of a total
(parsing, Spot, relaxation, reset completion) of 0.18, 2.2, 6.1, and 96~s, and the construction proper
(relaxation and reset completion) takes at most 6~s (R7;
\path{evaluation/stage_times.tsv}). For R8, Spot does not finish within
300~s (\path{evaluation/spot_low_timing.tsv}), so no $\mathsf{compile}(f)$ is built. A complete run of \texttt{mtl2tba} up to $\mathsf{compile}(f)$, which also
writes the DOT file, takes less than one second for every configuration except R5
(3.0~s), R6 (9.2~s), R7 (113~s), N6 (1.4~s), N7 (16~s), and N8 (85~s). These numbers describe the unoptimized output of the extracted
construction; they are not a comparison of tools. They are consistent with a cost of the
factorization: reusing a standard LTL-to-B\"uchi backend supports a modular
correctness argument but, with the backend used here, yielded larger automata
than CASAAL's direct construction. Reducing these sizes by post-processing is left to follow-up
work.

Printing the negated event atom explicitly does not change R and N, whose
formulas contain no negated event, and reduces the automata for F11--F14 (for example F11 from
5/11/2 to 4/7/2). On the 100 random formulas it changes 26 automata, all
smaller: the total of locations falls from 793 to 660, that of transitions from
3681 to 2305, and that of clocks from 163 to 159. Printing $\mathsf{LNAct}(a)$ as an independent atom
leaves cubes that require both $a$ and $\neg a$, which no
one-event-per-position word satisfies; the explicit printing used here does not produce them.

\subsection{Language checks with TChecker}
\label{subsec:tchecker}
For a formula $\varphi$, let $O$ and $O'$ be $\mathsf{compile}(\varphi)$ and
$\mathsf{compile}(\neg\varphi)$, and $C$, $C'$ CASAAL's automata for
$\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$. TChecker~0.8~\cite{tchecker} decides the
emptiness of the products $P_1=O\times C'$, $P_2=C\times O'$, and
$S=O\times O'$, built over infinite words with one event per position,
strictly increasing and divergent timestamps, and clocks starting at~0.
Empty $P_1$ and $P_2$ mean that $O$ is disjoint from $C'$ and $C$ from $O'$;
if CASAAL's two automata are complementary this gives
$L(O)\subseteq L(C)$ and $L(O')\subseteq L(C')$. The converse inclusions
would additionally require that $O$ and $O'$ together cover all words. An empty $S$ means that our automata for
$\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ never accept the same word. A fourth product
$O\times C$ is a sanity check: it must be non-empty when $\varphi$ is
satisfiable.

On the benchmark configurations, $P_1$, $P_2$, and $S$ are empty for every
configuration that completes within the limits (F1--F14 and F3b, except the hatted F3
and F13, which CASAAL cannot express; R1--R3; and N1--N4); for F3 and F13
only $S$ is checked, and it is empty. For R4--R7 and N5--N7 the three checks exceed 600~s. At 1800~s, run singly, $P_2$ of R4
is empty, while $P_1$, $S$ of R4 and all three checks of N5 still time out (which
may also mean memory exhaustion). For R8 no automaton is built within the limit.
For N8, $O$ is built (85~s), but $P_1$ exceeds 600~s and $O'$ is
not built within the limit, so $P_2$ and $S$ are not run (inconclusive).
The sanity product, computed for every completed configuration except the
hatted F3 and F13, is non-empty.

The artifact also contains 100 random formulas of depth at most three over
three propositions, generated with their CASAAL automata. For all 100 of them,
$P_1$, $P_2$, and $S$ are empty, with no timeout; for five of these, the
sanity product is empty as well, and inspection shows that each of these
five formulas is unsatisfiable under mutual exclusion (for example
$q\land\Diamond_{>2}p\land r$), so for the other 95 the empty products are not vacuous.

CASAAL prints some automata without any acceptance mark. We read such an
automaton by its state labels (a state with an undischarged unbounded
eventuality is not accepting), which is our interpretation of CASAAL's output,
not a documented convention; the verdicts of the products depend on this reading
for one random formula only.

\subsection{Agreement on ultimately periodic words}
The TChecker checks establish inclusions and time out on the larger
configurations, so we add a test of equality on finitely many words. For each
configuration we draw random ultimately periodic timed words $uv^\omega$ (a
prefix of 0--4 and a loop of 1--5 events, timestamps that are integer multiples of $0.5$,
strictly increasing and divergent; half of the words follow the order of the
propositions of the formula), and compare three verdicts: the formula under the pointwise semantics above, evaluated by a Python implementation that we
wrote independently of the translator (it is not extracted from, nor checked
against, the Rocq definitions);
acceptance by $\mathsf{compile}(f)$; and acceptance by CASAAL's automaton.
Clocks of CASAAL start at 0; for $\mathsf{compile}(f)$ initial clock values
are tried on a grid when it has at most three clocks, since the theorem
quantifies them existentially (Corollary~\ref{cor:zero-init} shows that zero
initial values give the same language); with more clocks, all clocks start
at~0. On 28 benchmark configurations (all but R6--R8;
400 words each, 11200 in all, 4495 satisfying the formula) and on the
100 random formulas (150 words each, 15000 in all, 8273 satisfying
it), the semantics and $\mathsf{compile}(f)$ agree on all sampled words. CASAAL is included on 26 of the
28 benchmark configurations (not on the hatted F3 and F13) and on all random
formulas, and agrees on all sampled words there as well: 10400 plus 15000
words, 25400 three-way comparisons; the other 800 words compare only the
semantics and the automaton. As a negative control, the same checker finds disagreements when F1
is compared with two mutated formulas, one with bound 4 instead of 5 (11 of 300
words) and one with a strict bound (5 of 300). The test has little weight for some formulas:
none of the random words satisfies the formula of N8, so for N5--N8 we
added five hand-built words each (accepted and rejected; all agree), and nine
random formulas have fewer than five satisfying and fourteen fewer than five
violating words. The words are finitely many and short; this is a test, not a
proof of equality.

\subsection{Backend contract}
Theorem \Rocqid{MTL_to_TBA_correct_with} assumes that the propositional
automaton accepts exactly the models of $\T(f)$. For each configuration we
saved the formula given to Spot and the LBTT automaton $A$ it returned, and
checked the contract with Spot's \texttt{autfilt}, using an independent
translator, ltl2ba~1.3: $A\times\mathrm{ltl2ba}(\neg\T(f))$ and
$\overline{A}\times\mathrm{ltl2ba}(\T(f))$, where $\overline{A}$ is the
complement of $A$, must both be empty. No discrepancy is
found on 28 of the 31 configurations; for R7 the equivalence check exceeds
600~s, for N8 ltl2ba exceeds 300~s, and for R8 Spot does not finish within
300~s. The acceptance marks of Spot's automaton are converted to the B\"uchi
acceptance of the Rocq record by the unverified LBTT reader, whose labels and
acceptance sets were checked only through the product tests above. On every configuration
where Spot produced an automaton, the number of states and transitions read by
the LBTT reader of \texttt{mtl2tba} equals the number reported by
\texttt{autfilt} on the same file. These checks test the contract on these
inputs; they do not prove it, and they use Spot's own tools for part of the
check.

\subsection{Hatted operators}
F3 and F13 use the hatted operators of Section~\ref{sec:logic}. CASAAL's input language has no
hatted operator; its closest encoding of F3,
$\Box(e\Rightarrow\Next\Box_{\le2}\neg e)$, measures the window from the next
event rather than from the triggering one
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hat-reference}) and is not equivalent, so we do not compare
the two languages. The two readings give different automata. For F3 and F13 the evidence is
limited to $S$ and to agreement with our Python evaluator, since no
independent tool expresses them. Under mutual exclusion the
non-strict window of the unhatted F3b contains the triggering event, so F3b
forbids $e$ altogether: $\mathsf{compile}(f)$ has one location, one
transition, and no clock. The window of F3 starts after the triggering event
but is measured from its timestamp, and $\mathsf{compile}(f)$ has two
locations, five transitions, and one clock $x$, reset on each $e$; after
an $e$, a further $e$ is accepted only with the guard $x>2$.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
The configurations and random formulas are small, of our choosing, and use
integer bounds only, so real-valued bounds are covered by the mathematical
theorem but not by the extracted tool or by any test. Test timestamps lie on a half-unit grid, so behavior at finer
offsets is not exercised. The TChecker products and,
for formulas with more than three clocks, the word-level test start all clocks
at 0; by Corollary~\ref{cor:zero-init}, for this translation zero initial clocks
accept the same language as existentially chosen ones. The R family shows that the size of Spot's automaton grows quickly with the
number of conjuncts. The CASAAL automata were produced before this study and not
regenerated; CASAAL's integer bounds, nondecreasing time, and acceptance
printing differ from our model, and the inclusions depend on the unchecked
complementarity of its automata. The TChecker products, the word-level test,
and the conversion scripts are unverified code. Timeouts are reported as
inconclusive.
